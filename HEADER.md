# China OSINT

Public records, open data, research databases and tools for investigating stories about China, including Hong Kong and Macau. Every link was checked on the date shown beside it.

Many mainland government sites are slow or block visitors from outside China, and some Western research sites block automated checks, so a source marked that way may still work for you in a browser.

## How to read the tables

- **Access** says what stands between you and the data: *Open* (nothing), *Free login*, *Captcha* (free, but a captcha on each search), *Paid*, or *Partly paid*.
- **Run by** tells you whose data it is. A government portal, a company, a think tank and a volunteer group each have different reasons to be right or wrong.
- **Checked** is the date we last opened the link. Portals move and die often, and Chinese platforms remove content without notice. If the date is old, check the link before relying on it.
- Notes in *italics* are caveats we hit while testing.

## What is deliberately left out

This list covers records about companies, government, courts, procurement, patents, land, the environment, infrastructure, the military and human rights. It does not include services that look up a private person's phone number, address or identity documents, or anything built on leaked or breached personal data. The one exception is clearly marked: police files that were leaked and then published by researchers, which are a documented public research source.

## Using these sources well

- **A record shows what was filed, not what is true.** Company filings, court notices and procurement documents are claims made by the filer.
- **Archive what you find.** Chinese pages and posts disappear quickly. Save them to the Wayback Machine or archive.today and keep a dated copy.
- **Names are not unique, and transliterations vary.** Match on a second detail (a unified social credit code, an address, a date) before saying two records are the same person or company.
- **Think about safety.** Searching, downloading or contacting sources can carry risks for people inside China. Don't put sources or colleagues at risk, and check the law before you scrape.
